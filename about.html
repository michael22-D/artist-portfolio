Artist Statement

Derrick Akuamoah is a Ghanaian abstract mixed-media artist based in Accra, Ghana. His artistic practice explores the overlooked marks, textures, and material traces embedded within everyday environments, examining how surfaces record time, movement, human activity, memory, and transformation.

Working primarily with acrylic, charcoal, pastel, and mixed-media materials, Akuamoah approaches painting as an ongoing process of observation, experimentation, and discovery. Rather than beginning with predetermined images, he allows the accumulation of layers, gestures, scratches, stains, erasures, and textured surfaces to guide the evolution of each composition. Through processes of adding, covering, removing, and revealing, his works emerge through an intuitive dialogue between the artist and the materials.

Akuamoah views these traces as more than physical marks. They are quiet records of existence that carry fragments of memory, presence, experience, and human interaction. Through abstraction, he transforms ordinary and often overlooked surfaces into spaces for reflection, revealing how seemingly insignificant details can hold deeper meanings.

His work invites viewers to reconsider the unnoticed aspects of their surroundings and recognize the significance of marks that often escape attention. Through their accumulation, interruption, and transformation, these traces suggest movement, human presence, and collective memory while remaining open to individual interpretation.

Rooted in the belief that "nothing truly disappears; everything leaves a trace," Akuamoah's practice seeks to uncover and reimagine what remains beneath the surface, giving renewed visibility and meaning to the silent impressions of everyday life.

Artistic Philosophy

“Nothing truly disappears; everything leaves a trace”.

Ordinary and overlooked surfaces carry silent stories of memory, time, and human presence. Through abstraction and mixed-media experimentation, I reinterpret scratches, stains, cracks, and weathered textures as expressions of memory, perception, and presence.

Artist Process

I build layers through adding, covering, scraping, removing, and revealing. As the painting progresses, I allow materials and spontaneous interactions to guide it. The process is as significant as the finished work: observation, experimentation, and discovery.
